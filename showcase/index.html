      Journal · Big Sur Coast

      Three days among the kelp

      By Ines Marlow · 7 min read

      Short on time?
      Uses your browser's built-in Summarizer when it has one. Nothing leaves your device.

      We launched from Monastery Beach at first light, while the swell was still asleep. Below the hull the water turned from slate to bottle green, and then the kelp began: tall golden stalks swaying in a current we could barely feel.

      Giant kelp grows up to sixty centimetres a day. Paddle through it slowly and you hear it before you see anything else: the gulp of sea otters cracking urchins on their bellies, the hiss of fronds against the bow.

      Day two: the long crossing

      The forecast promised eight knots from the north-west and delivered fifteen. Our guide, Marco, split the group into two pods, moved the slower boats into the lee of the cliffs and kept up a running commentary about cormorant nests to keep minds off sore shoulders.

      By noon we had crossed the bay. Lunch was fish tacos on a beach that only appears at low tide, and a nap that lasted exactly as long as the tide allowed.

      Day three: otters, and a lesson in patience

      On the last morning we drifted for an hour without paddling at all. A mother otter and her pup floated twenty metres off, wrapped in kelp so they wouldn't drift apart while they slept. Nobody spoke. Nobody needed to.

      If you go, pack a warm hat even in July, eat the second taco, and let the guide choose the route. The coast has been doing this for a long time; it knows the way.
